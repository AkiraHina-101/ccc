Surface Split

This tool separates the boundary faces of a sealed 3D-mesh assembly into OUTER, INNER, HOLE and CONTACT. It helps distinguish the skin seen while rotating the model from cavity walls, true drilled bores, and faces mated to another part.

Quick usage

  1. Analyze a new model. Browse to the BDF and press Run Analyzer (~1 min, background). A folder named for the model is created beside the BDF; when finished, its results are loaded automatically.
  2. Load a previous result. Browse to the *.outer_index.json file and press Load and validate JSON.
  3. Classify and select. Press Classify All once. Select a part, then press OUTER, INNER, HOLE, or CONTACT to highlight that group. To understand picked faces, press Debug selected faces.
  4. Handle an exception. Pick faces and force OUTER, INNER, HOLE, or CONTACT; use Clear override on selection to remove it. Overrides are for rare physical cases the automatic evidence cannot reveal and are remembered with this result set. Reclassify after an override so the surrounding regions are updated.

The four classes

OUTER

The exterior skin: faces you can see from outside when you rotate the assembled model. A visible face can lead the surrounding continuous skin into OUTER.

INNER

Walls of an internal cavity. They are reached from the inside rather than from outside; CONTACT boundaries and the exterior skin separate them from OUTER.

HOLE

A real drilled or bored cylindrical wall. It may be one closed cylindrical face or several coaxial pieces that become a closed bore when joined.

CONTACT

A face pressed against or shared with another part: a mating face. This includes the directly attached, flat/coplanar ring around a shared face when that ring is physically pressed. A closed rim—such as a chamfer or bolt-seat ring—is an ordinary face, not HOLE and not CONTACT.

Debug field glossary

Debug values are face shares from 0 to 1, printed as 0–100%, unless stated otherwise. A dash means that no applicable gate or hole route was found.

How the filters decide

HOLE: upright, hollow cylindrical wall

Every single-face hole must have radius ≤45 mm (or ≤60 mm only if coverage ≥0.95, cv ≤0.05 and tilt ≤0.30), tilt ≤0.55, hollow ≥0.35, and conc ≤0.95. A shallow tilted frustum is rejected when tilt ≥0.30 and length/radius <0.20.

RouteRequirements after the common guards
Tier 1Closed radial loop; ne ≥8, cv ≤0.20, cover ≥0.80, inw ≥0.60.
Tier 2Closed radial loop; ne ≥40, cv ≤0.05, cover ≥0.65, inw ≥0.90.
Tier 3 deep tubene ≥50, cv ≤0.05, cover ≥0.42, length ≥2.5r, inw ≥0.95. Depth is sufficient evidence, so this is the only tier that need not be closed by itself.
Tier 4Closed radial loop; ne ≥30, cv ≤0.02, cover ≥0.52, inw ≥0.95.
ring0Closed upright one-row bore: ne ≥8, conc ≤0.40, tilt ≤0.30, cv ≤0.35, hollow ≥0.50, inw ≥0.60.

“Closed” here means the radial normal remainder is ≤0.45. Separate fragments are grouped only when their axes align (cosine ≥0.90), radii differ by at most 25%, and centres match within max(30% of radius, 2 mm). The joined group is a HOLE only when its normal concentration is ≤0.45. Ordinary fragments need ne ≥14, nin ≥1, cv ≤0.45, r ≤45, inw ≥0.80, tilt ≤0.50 and hollow ≥0.35. A stricter micro/deep route permits ne ≥4 only with cv ≤0.08, tilt ≤0.30, hollow ≥0.85 and either r ≤6 mm or length ≥0.8r. This prevents a shallow bolt-seat strip from becoming a hole.

A closed rim is protected as ordinary: r ≤45, ring cv ≤0.35, inward ≥0.60, hollow ≥0.45, centroid offset ≤0.35r, at least 5 occupied angular bins, and no angular gap above 4 of 24 bins (60°).

CONTACT: shared or pressed mating face

Final CONTACT begins with faces actually shared by parts, after HOLE and closed rims are removed. It then adds only one directly attached coplanar layer: normals must have |cosine| ≥0.94 (about 20°) and plane separation ≤3 mm. The added face must be pressed: shn ≥50%, or ct_in ≥60% with an interior element, or ct ≥60% and at least its vis/vis30. If reach ≥80%, the last route instead needs ct ≥80%. A visible coplanar face stays OUTER.

The displayed diagnostic gates explain supporting evidence: shared nodes ≥98%; pressed interior n_in ≥3 and ct_in ≥60%; or ct ≥80%, reach ≤20%, and shn=0. A proximity cluster is kept only if it touches a core/pressed candidate.

OUTER and INNER: continuous physical regions

OUTER starts from vis ≥20% and grows through ordinary adjacent faces. HOLE and CONTACT are barriers. If a disconnected shell has no close-120 seed, vis30 ≥20% supplies its seed. Only the largest grown OUTER component is retained in each disconnected shell; a second visible floating piece is treated as INNER.

A leftover patch touching grown OUTER is filled when it also touches a HOLE or has any vis, vis30, or reach evidence. A HOLE-cut isolated patch may be rescued only if it has a face with vis or vis30 ≥20% and either mean visibility ≥28%, or mean visibility ≥23% plus mean reach ≥80% and ≥70% of its area with reach ≥80%. INNER is then the main remaining interior region per shell, grown without crossing OUTER or CONTACT.

Core algorithm — technical view

This is the execution path behind one dataset. The Analyzer produces element-level evidence; Surface Split converts that evidence to face ratios and combines it with live mesh topology and fitted geometry.

  1. Analyzer, two standard passes. The background run invokes the fast analyzer with --all and the parallel engine. It reads GRID and CTRIA3 records from the BDF, preserving the part mapping. Pass A is 5 mm voxels with 30 mm closing. Pass B is 3 mm voxels with 120 mm closing, suffix _split, and 10/20/40 mm reach snapshots.
  2. Voxel evidence. For each pass, triangles are voxelized. The closed occupancy is flood-filled from outside; raw open space is then grown from that outside region to measure air reach. The analyzer also marks voxels occupied by triangles from different parts. Visibility is calculated by orthographic z-buffer projection from 128 Fibonacci-sphere directions. A triangle becomes visible, contact-proximity, or reachable when its occupied voxels meet the respective mask; element IDs are emitted by part.
  3. Dataset and mapping. The analyzer writes visible, contact, reach, and optional reach-level element sets plus an index beside the BDF. For tetrahedral models, the triangle-to-parent-element map keeps BDF evidence aligned with live boundary faces. At classification time, every face ratio is the count of its mapped elements in a set divided by its mapped element count. The required inputs are split visibility, split reach, and close-30 visibility.
  4. Live face model. For each selected body, the tool builds or reuses geometry: face nodes, edge/node adjacency, interior elements, and cylindrical-fit features. It reads shared faces and shared nodes from the live assembly. It then detects HOLE faces, detects protected closed rims, and creates CONTACT from native shared faces plus one pressed coplanar layer.
  5. Topology classification. OUTER seeds are faces with vis ≥0.20. They flood through adjacency but cannot cross HOLE or CONTACT; a shell with no close-120 seed may use vis30 ≥0.20. Island fill and the narrow area-weighted rescue are applied, then only the largest OUTER component in each disconnected shell is retained. INNER starts from the largest remaining interior component per shell and grows without crossing OUTER or CONTACT.
  6. Finalization. The result stores face IDs, diagnostics, and a cache token per body. Selecting a class reuses this cache. A manual OUTER/INNER override becomes a seed/exclusion; a manual HOLE/CONTACT override becomes a barrier. The cache is invalidated and classification reflows around that override on the next Classify All.

Tips & safety

  • Classify once; later Select actions use the cache and are immediate.
  • Parts with the same name share analysis evidence. Review duplicated-name parts together.
  • The analyzer runs again only when you press it; an existing result can simply be loaded.
  • Overrides are saved per result dataset. Use them for known physical exceptions, then reclassify.
  • Inspect uncertain faces with Debug before forcing a class; a forced HOLE or CONTACT changes the boundary around which OUTER and INNER flow.
